\begin{abstract}
A large instruction-tuned model (Gemma~4 31B), given a heavily scaffolded
prompt (an author style profile in prose, a vocabulary palette, exemplar
lines and explicit structural targets), can rewrite a Macedonian poem in a
named poet's style. This report covers the attempt to obtain the same
behaviour from a small, locally runnable model that is given only the source
poem and the target author's name. The teacher's outputs on 1{,}146
train-split pairs are distilled into Qwen3-4B by LoRA supervised fine-tuning
(SFT); the distilled model is then trained further with Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO) against a reward built from the project's own
stylometric profiles, a line-level content-similarity score and
multiplicative gates against copying, repetition and malformed output. SFT
teaches the task format completely but collapses ten target voices into one
averaged voice dominated by the corpus's two largest authors. GRPO (v1)
raises the reward from 0.254 to 0.406, removes the repetition SFT showed
under plain sampling and doubles content preservation, but it halves
author identification (on the reward classifier and, less sharply, on a held-out
judge) and learns to copy single source lines at the edge of the copy gate.
The Gemma teacher, scored on the same pairs, is still identified as the
requested author three to eight times as often as either student. GRPO-v2 changes only the reward to address both findings,
and a second student of matched size from another family (Gemma~4 E4B)
separates properties of Qwen from properties of the method. GRPO-v2 keeps
author identification close to SFT's on both classifiers instead of losing
half of it, but only by holding the policy on the two largest authors, not by
learning the other eight, and it gives back most of v1's repetition fix; for eight of the ten
targets neither student is ever identified as the requested author, which
leaves GRPO's group-relative update nothing to amplify.

\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}\label{sec:overview}

The project's corpus is 2{,}711 Macedonian poems and song lyrics (Cyrillic).
Two earlier style-transfer approaches precede this work: a token-substitution
pipeline that splices author vocabulary into masked slots, and a generative
pipeline that sends Gemma~4 31B one prompt combining an English summary of
the source poem, its content keywords, the target author's style profile in
prose, a relevance-filtered vocabulary palette, tone-exemplar lines and
structural targets (lines, stanzas, tokens per line). The second produces
fluent, recognisably author-specific poems, but it depends on a 31B model
behind a rate-limited API (about three minutes per poem including the
model's obligatory reasoning) and on the whole stylometric pipeline at
inference time.

The question this report addresses is how close a 4B model can get to that
teacher when it is given only
\begin{center}
\emph{source poem} $+$ \emph{target author's name} $\;\to\;$ \emph{new poem}.
\end{center}
The work is three stages, each consuming the previous one's output:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Synthetic data} (\S\ref{sec:data}). The teacher pipeline,
    unmodified, writes transfers for (source, target) pairs drawn from the
    train split only.
  \item \textbf{SFT} (\S\ref{sec:sft}, \S\ref{sec:sfteval}). LoRA
    fine-tuning of Qwen3-4B on those pairs, in a compact chat format that
    contains none of the teacher's scaffolding.
  \item \textbf{GRPO} (\S\ref{sec:grpo}--\S\ref{sec:grpov2}). Reward-driven
    fine-tuning of the SFT model against a reward computed only from the
    generated poem, the source poem and the target author.
\end{enumerate}
\S\ref{sec:gemma} repeats stages 2 and 3 with a second student
(Gemma~4 E4B) to test whether the failures found with Qwen belong to the
student or to the method.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Splits}

\texttt{make\_splits.py} assigns every poem to \texttt{train},
\texttt{val} or \texttt{test}, 80/10/10 per author, seeded (seed 42). The
unit is the poem, not the (author, title) pair, which is not unique in this
corpus (Блаже Конески alone has four distinct poems titled \texttt{ПЕСНА}).
Authors with fewer than three poems cannot be split three ways and go
entirely to \texttt{train}; every other author has at least one poem in each
split. Over the 1{,}239 poems of the 38 profiled authors this gives 987
train, 126 val and 126 test poems. Every later stage reads the split from
this one file; nothing recomputes it.

\subsection{Target authors}

The ten target authors are the ten authors with the most poems among those
with a style profile (ties broken alphabetically): Блаже Конески, Пијан
Славеј, Ефтим Клетников, Ристо Лазаров, Веле Смилевски, Братислав Ташковски,
Влада Урошевиќ, Адем Гајтани, Славко Јаневски and Катица Ќулавкова. They
were chosen for having enough real poems to profile reliably, not by a
diversity criterion; distinct voices follow from their being different
poets.

\subsection{Synthetic training data}

\texttt{generate\_synthetic.py} calls the teacher pipeline
(\texttt{llm\_style\_transfer.run\_transfer}) in a loop over sampled
(source, target) pairs from the train split, with one sample per pair. The
same set of source poems is reused across all target authors, so that the
training data contrasts authors on identical content rather than on
different poems. The run produced 1{,}146 examples: 130 distinct source
poems, 108--120 examples per target author, with self-transfer pairs
skipped and failed pairs (API errors, empty answers) logged separately
rather than retried into the data.

Generation is the throughput bottleneck: the Gemini API serves a few
requests per minute, and Gemma~4 always reasons before answering, with
reasoning length varying between about 2k and 16k tokens for the same
prompt. The batch was therefore run as one process per target author in
parallel, each writing its own part file, merged afterwards on the key
(source poem, target author, sample index). The output file is also the
resume record, so an interrupted run never regenerates a finished pair.

The same procedure on the val split (50 val poems $\times$ 10 targets, self
pairs skipped, 468 pairs) gives the teacher's output on exactly the pairs
the students are evaluated on (\texttt{data/synthetic/teacher\_val.csv}).
It is evaluation data only and is never trained on. All 468 pairs are
complete.

\section{Supervised fine-tuning}\label{sec:sft}

\subsection{Task format}

The student is trained on a deliberately minimal chat:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{system}: ``You rewrite Macedonian poems in a different poet's
    style. Given a source poem and a target author, write a new poem in
    Macedonian, in Cyrillic script, that conveys the source poem's content
    and imagery in the target author's voice.''
  \item \emph{user}: \texttt{Source poem:\textbackslash n\{poem\}\textbackslash n\textbackslash nTarget author: \{name\}}
  \item \emph{assistant}: the teacher's poem.
\end{itemize}
None of the teacher's scaffolding --- the summary, keywords, style profile,
palette, exemplars or structural targets --- is in the student's prompt.
Whatever the student knows about an author it has to learn from the 108--120
teacher poems written for that author. The same module
(\texttt{sft\_data.py}) renders the prompt for training, for validation
generation and for GRPO, through the tokenizer's own chat template, so the
three cannot drift. Qwen3 is used as a non-reasoning model
(\texttt{enable\_thinking=False}).

\subsection{Training}

LoRA on \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-4B} with TRL's \texttt{SFTTrainer} and PEFT:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
LoRA rank / $\alpha$ / dropout & 16 / 32 / 0.05 \\
Target modules & \texttt{q,k,v,o,gate,up,down\_proj} (all attention and MLP projections) \\
Epochs & 3 \\
Batch & 4 per device $\times$ 4 accumulation $=16$ \\
Learning rate & $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine, 3\,\% warm-up \\
Max sequence length & 1024 tokens \\
Precision & bf16, gradient checkpointing \\
Loss & assistant turn only (\texttt{assistant\_only\_loss}) \\
Seed & 42 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The target modules were checked against the transformers Qwen3 source rather
than taken from a generic Llama default; Qwen3's \texttt{q\_norm} and
\texttt{k\_norm} are RMSNorm layers and are not adapted. The loss is masked
to the assistant turn: TRL patches generation markers into Qwen3's chat
template for this. With that template the masked span also contains Qwen3's
constant empty reasoning block (\texttt{<think>\textbackslash n\textbackslash n</think>}) and the newline after
the end-of-turn token, which are trivially learnable and do not affect the
poem.

prompts included ($6.1\times10^{16}$ FLOPs). The loss, over assistant tokens
only, from the run's \texttt{trainer\_state.json}:

70  & 1.00 & 1.717 & 0.583 & 1.70 \\
80  & 1.14 & 1.572 & 0.605 & 1.60 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
{\footnotesize Each row is the mean over the preceding 10 steps. Token
accuracy is the share of assistant tokens predicted exactly; entropy is the
mean entropy of the predicted next-token distribution.}

Most of the loss reduction happens in the first epoch (2.87 $\to$ 1.72).
After that, the loss falls in steps at the epoch boundaries and stays almost
flat within an epoch: it drops 0.15 from step 70 to step 80 and 0.13 from
step 140 to step 150, but only 0.03 over the 60 steps of the third epoch
after step 150. Steps at epoch boundaries are the usual sign that the model
recognises examples it has already seen. With 1{,}146 examples and three
passes, part of the second and third epochs' gain is memorisation of the
training poems rather than better generalisation. The run has no held-out
loss to tell the two apart: the evaluation split was configured, but the
synthetic dataset contains only train rows, so no evaluation step ran. The
teacher's val-pair outputs (\texttt{teacher\_val.csv},
\S\ref{sec:teacherval}) were generated later and could now serve as that
held-out set. The final loss of 1.34 corresponds to a per-token perplexity of
about 3.8 on the teacher's poems.

The run took 881\,s (about 15 minutes) on one NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB.

\subsection{Runtime environment}

Training and generation run on a single A100 in a Slurm cluster, inside a
Singularity image built from the project's own \texttt{pyproject.toml} and
\texttt{uv.lock} (\texttt{uv sync --frozen} at build time). The image is
therefore the same environment as local \texttt{uv run}, with no separate
CUDA base image: the PyPI \texttt{torch} wheel bundles its CUDA runtime, and
only the driver comes from the host (\texttt{--nv}). One bind mount maps the
repository into the container, so a job reads and writes the same
\texttt{data/} and \texttt{models/} as local runs.

\section{Evaluation methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Protocol}

Every model is evaluated on the same 468 val pairs (50 val poems $\times$
10 targets, self pairs skipped), with the same prompt, the same per-item
seeds and plain sampling unless stated otherwise ($T=0.7$, top-$p=0.9$, top-$k$
20, at most 512 new tokens). Models compared with each other are generated
back to back in one job on one GPU: sampling draws from the device's own RNG
stream, so comparing a CPU run with a GPU run would confound the model
difference with a change of random generator. Results are reported over all
rows and over the \emph{paired} view (only pairs present in every compared
run).

\subsection{Metrics}

All metrics are computed by one module, \texttt{style\_metrics.py}, and are
oriented higher-is-better and mostly bounded in $[0,1]$, because the GRPO
reward uses the same functions; a reward that disagreed with the evaluation
metric would be a defect.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{3.4cm}p{10.8cm}}
\toprule
Group & Metrics \\
\midrule
Degeneracy & distinct-1/2/3; line uniqueness; maximum repeats of one line.
  An output is \emph{degenerate} if distinct-1 $<0.40$ or line uniqueness
  $<0.80$. \\
Format & Cyrillic ratio; no markdown or commentary. \\
Structure & fit of line count, stanza count and tokens per line to the
  target author's profile. \\
Target style & \texttt{classifier.pkl} probability of and top-1 for the target
  author; target TF-IDF vocabulary hit rate; the held-out judge
  (\S\ref{sec:judge}). \\
Content & content-word recall from the source; line novelty and copy
  novelty (\S\ref{sec:copy}). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two reference rows anchor the numbers: the teacher's output, and the target
authors' real poems (\texttt{--corpus-reference}), which show what each
metric scores on human text.

\subsection{Copying has to be measured}\label{sec:copy}

The untuned base model's dominant response is not a failed style transfer.
It reproduces the source poem verbatim under a markdown heading, followed by
a paragraph explaining the target author's style. That output has
near-perfect content preservation and, because it is real human poetry,
excellent diversity and fluency. Under any combination of diversity, fluency
and content metrics, ``change nothing'' would score highest.
\texttt{line\_novelty} (share of generated lines that are not verbatim source
lines after normalising punctuation and case) and its fuzzier successor
\texttt{copy\_novelty} (\S\ref{sec:gates}) are the guard.

\subsection{Two author classifiers}\label{sec:judge}

\texttt{classifier.pkl} is the project's character-(2,4)-gram author
classifier. It was fitted on the whole corpus, so on the target authors' real
poems it scores top-1 1.000 by memorisation; the same recipe's held-out
accuracy under 5-fold cross-validation, 0.883, is the honest ceiling.
GRPO also uses this classifier's target probability as a reward component,
so after GRPO its score is no longer independent evidence: a policy trained
to raise it will raise it whether or not the poem reads as the author.

The \emph{judge} (\texttt{judge\_classifier.py}) is a second classifier used
only for evaluation and differs from the reward classifier in data, features
and model: it is fitted on train-split poems only (authors with at least
five train poems, 23 authors, 963 poems), on word unigrams and bigrams, with
multinomial logistic regression. Its top-1 on the 172 val and test poems of
the ten target authors is 0.663, which is the ceiling for real unseen poems;
in the real-poem row the judge's columns are blank for train poems, which it
has seen. A test forbids importing the judge from any reward module. A gain
in classifier top-1 without a gain in judge top-1 would mean the reward
classifier was gamed.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning worked on every axis the task is defined on.} The
tuned model writes original lines instead of copying (1.000 vs.\ 0.224),
never emits markdown or commentary (1.000 vs.\ 0.592), fits the target
author's structure better (0.848 vs.\ 0.680), and is 15 times more likely to
be identified as the requested author (0.141 vs.\ 0.009). Its content-word
recall, 0.173, matches the teacher's 0.176: the teacher's degree of content
preservation was distilled faithfully, low as it is.

\paragraph{The base model's diversity advantage is an artifact.} 87.8\,\% of
its outputs are mostly copied from the source, and the copied human poetry
carries the diversity. On the 12.2\,\% of rows where it composes something
new, its distinct-1 is 0.532 --- indistinguishable from the tuned model's
0.515 --- with a classifier top-1 of 0.035. Fine-tuning did not cost
diversity; it removed the copying that disguised the base model's own lack
of it.

falls into a fixed line template, $\langle$noun$\rangle$

its best-scoring output (classifier probability 0.785 for Пијан Славеј, a
poem in which \emph{Мераци брзо се вртат сега} occurs four times).

\subsection{Degenerate decoding versus a degenerate policy}\label{sec:decodeablation}

The repetition and the collapse could be one fault or two. A decoding
ablation separates them: the same adapter, 56 val pairs, the same seeds,
four decoding settings.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
& Plain & Repetition & Repetition & No repeated \\
Metric & sampling & penalty 1.1 & penalty 1.2 & 4-gram \\
\midrule
distinct-1                    & 0.503 & 0.807 & 0.895 & 0.751 \\
line uniqueness               & 0.876 & 0.998 & 1.000 & 1.000 \\
degenerate outputs            & 30.4\% & \textbf{0.0\%} & \textbf{0.0\%} & \textbf{0.0\%} \\
\midrule
classifier top-1 = target     & 0.125 & 0.107 & 0.161 & 0.125 \\
\quad paired change vs.\ plain & ---   & $-0.018$ & $+0.036$ & $+0.000$ \\
\midrule

structural fit                & 0.859 & 0.837 & 0.813 & 0.837 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The repetition is a decoding artifact.} A repetition penalty of
1.1 removes degenerate output entirely and lifts distinct-1 from 0.503 to
0.807, past both the teacher and the real poems. Banning repeated 4-grams
does the same at 0.751, closest to human text.

\paragraph{The style collapse is not.} None of the settings moves target-style
match: paired changes in classifier top-1 are noise around zero. That deficit
is in the policy, and only training can move it.

\paragraph{Cost.} A repetition penalty cannot tell a looped word from a word
legitimately carried over from the source, so content recall falls (0.125
$\to$ 0.078 at 1.1, $\to$ 0.031 at 1.2). The 4-gram ban constrains sequences
and costs less (0.088), at the price of forbidding deliberate refrains, which
real poems use (line uniqueness 0.969).

rerun on all 468 val pairs for both the base and the SFT model
(\texttt{data/validation\_generations/reppen-1.1/}):

The 56-pair result holds at full scale. The penalty removes the SFT model's
repetition (line uniqueness 0.999, at most 1.03 repeats of a line) at a cost
of a third of its content recall. On 468 pairs it also raises classifier
top-1 from 0.141 to 0.182, a larger move than the noise-level changes of the
56-pair ablation, and the held-out judge moves the same way (0.158 $\to$
0.175); the TF-IDF hit rate almost doubles as well, consistent with
the penalty pushing the model off its few looped words and onto a wider
vocabulary that includes more of the target's. Under the penalty the base
model still copies most of its source (line novelty 0.361) and is no better
at the task. All GRPO comparisons below use plain sampling, so that the
policy, not the decoder, is what is being measured.

\subsection{Defects found during the evaluation}

\paragraph{Validation generation ran on the CPU.}
\texttt{from\_pretrained} places the model on the CPU and \texttt{generate()}
then runs there silently. The first validation run took 131\,s per
generation (17 hours for 468 rows) on a node with an idle A100, against
15.5\,s once the model is moved to the device. The loader now logs the device
the model is on.

\paragraph{6.2\,\% of training examples were truncated.} Measured with the
Qwen3 tokenizer, the 1{,}146 training examples have median length 622 tokens,
99th percentile 2{,}364 and maximum 4{,}031; at \texttt{max\_seq\_length}
1024, 71 examples (6.2\,\%) lose the end of their assistant turn and with it
the end-of-turn token, so they train the model to continue past where a poem
should stop. This is a plausible contributor to the looping. 2048 would cover
about 99\,\% of examples. Qwen's SFT was \emph{not} retrained at 2048,
because that would change the start policy of both GRPO runs; it is a stated
limitation (\S\ref{sec:limits}), and the Gemma student is trained at 4096
tokens, which truncates nothing (\S\ref{sec:gemma}).

\subsection{What SFT leaves for GRPO}

Repetition needs a decoding control or a policy change, not more SFT.
Style collapse is what the GRPO stage exists to address: SFT taught the task
format but not the separation between authors. The style reward therefore
has to be computed per target author, so that a policy reverting to Конески
is not paid for it, and content has to be a reward term in its own right,
because imitating a teacher that preserves little content cannot raise it.

\section{GRPO: method and reward}\label{sec:grpo}

GRPO (TRL's \texttt{GRPOTrainer}) samples $G$ completions per prompt, scores
each with the reward, and normalises rewards within the group, so the update
pushes probability towards the above-average members of each group. There is
no value network; the group mean is the baseline. The reward sees three
inputs only: the generated poem, the source poem and the target author. The
teacher's output is never a reward target, and no val or test poem enters
the prompts, the reward calibration or any in-training evaluation.

\subsection{Why the SFT adapter is merged first}

With a LoRA policy, TRL obtains the KL reference by switching the trainable
adapter off. Continuing to train the SFT adapter would therefore anchor the
KL term to the \emph{untuned} base model, the one that copies its source
under a markdown heading. Instead the SFT adapter is merged into the base
weights (\texttt{merge\_sft\_adapter.py}, which records the adapter's hash)
and a fresh LoRA is trained on top. A fresh LoRA starts as the identity
($B=0$), so the start policy and the KL reference are both exactly the SFT
model. The merged model reproduces the unmerged adapter's validation scores
to within sampling noise ($R=0.254$ for both).

\subsection{Prompts}

Train-split poems $\times$ the ten targets, self-transfer skipped, seeded
shuffle, rendered with the SFT prompt format. Prompts longer than 1{,}024
tokens are dropped, never truncated.

\subsection{Style reward}

The style reward reuses the definitions of the per-author style profiles,
computed per poem. The per-poem code was extracted into
\texttt{style\_features.py}, which the profiler now imports; the refactored
profiler's output is byte-identical to the original's. Generated poems are
tagged with \texttt{tagging.tag\_poem}, the corpus build's tagger with its
hand corrections and filtering.

Every feature is scored against the target author's \emph{per-poem}
distribution over their train poems (\texttt{reward\_calibration.json}),
because a single poem varies a lot around its author's mean. For a scalar
feature $x_f$,
\begin{equation}
  s_f = \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 z_f^2\right),\qquad
  z_f = \frac{x_f-\mu_{a,f}}{\max(\sigma_{a,f},\,0.5\,\sigma_{\mathrm{corpus},f})},
\end{equation}
where the floor stops authors with few or unusually uniform poems from
zeroing every small miss. For the POS-bigram and rhyme-ending distributions,
the cosine $c$ to the author's aggregate distribution is scored one-sidedly
against the author's own leave-one-out self-similarity $(\mu_c,\sigma_c)$:
$s=1$ for $c\ge\mu_c$, otherwise $\exp(-\tfrac12((c-\mu_c)/\sigma_c)^2)$. The
classifier's target probability is used as it is, and TF-IDF palette hits are
divided by the author's 90th-percentile hit rate on real poems (the raw rate
is about 0.05 even for human text). Scores are averaged within groups and
$R_{\mathrm{style}}$ is the weighted mean of the groups:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Group & Features & v1 weight & v2 weight \\
\midrule
POS profile & \texttt{pct\_NOUN/VERB/ADJ/ADV/PROPN} & 1.0 & 1.0 \\
lexical diversity & MATTR over lemmas (TTR below the 200 window) & 1.0 & 1.0 \\
structure & tokens/line, lines, stanzas, lines/stanza & 1.0 & 1.0 \\
POS bigrams & bigram distribution & 1.0 & 1.0 \\
classifier & \texttt{clf\_target\_prob} & 1.0 & \textbf{5.6} \\
rhyme & rhyme-ending distribution & 0.5 & 0.5 \\
TF-IDF & palette hits / p90 & 0.5 & 0.5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Content reward}

Content is a line-level, BERTScore-style F1 against the source poem over
\texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2} embeddings: recall averages
each source line's best cosine match among the generated lines, precision the
reverse. Working per line avoids the model's 128-token limit and stops a poem
that covers only the first stanza from earning full recall. Because every line
takes its best match, unrelated poems already score high: the mean F1 over
2{,}000 random cross-author train pairs is $b=0.553$. The reward is rescaled
so that this level is zero, $R_{\mathrm{content}}=\mathrm{clip}\big((F_1-b)/(1-b),0,1\big)$.

\subsection{Gates}\label{sec:gates}

Three behaviours are cheaper for the policy than doing the task. Each is
gated multiplicatively rather than added as another objective, so it cannot
be traded against the rest of the reward:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Copying.} $R_{\mathrm{content}}$ is multiplied by
    \texttt{copy\_novelty}, the share of generated lines that are not
    near-copies. A line of at least three tokens is a copy if at least
    75\,\% of its word types occur in one source line. Containment is used
    rather than Jaccard because on this corpus's $\sim$5-token lines one
    swapped word gives Jaccard $4/6=0.67$, which a 0.7 threshold would let
    through. v2 raises the gate to a power, \texttt{copy\_novelty}$^k$, $k=3$
    (\S\ref{sec:grpov2}).
  \item \emph{Repetition.} $R_{\mathrm{style}}$ is multiplied by line
    uniqueness (0.97 on real poems).
  \item \emph{Format.} Both terms are multiplied by
    $\mathrm{validity}=\texttt{is\_cyrillic}\cdot\texttt{no\_chat\_markup}\cdot\texttt{is\_nonempty}$.
    The markup test is narrower than the reported \texttt{no\_markup}: bullet
    and numbered-line patterns fire on 49 of the 1{,}239 real poems (dialogue
    lines opening with ``- ''), and as a hard gate zeroed a real target poem
    in the fixtures. Bold, heading and code-fence markup alone catch 186 of
    the untuned model's 191 markup outputs and occur in one real poem.
\end{itemize}
The total is
$R=\alpha\,R_{\mathrm{style}}^{g}+\beta\,R_{\mathrm{content}}^{g}$, where $g$
marks the gated terms.

\subsection{Choosing the weights from within-group spread}

Under group-normalised advantages, which term drives the update depends on
its spread \emph{within} a group, not on its mean. \texttt{evaluate\_reward.py
--groups} samples $G=4$ SFT completions per train prompt exactly as training
does and reports each component's within-group standard deviation
(\texttt{data/grpo/reward\_groups.csv}). The gated style term's is 0.113 and
the gated content term's only 0.042, so at equal weights style would
dominate the advantage almost three to one. The training runs therefore use
$\alpha=0.3$, $\beta=0.7$. Within style, the classifier's spread is 0.051,
against 0.26 and 0.31 for lexical diversity and POS bigrams; this
measurement is what v2's classifier weight is derived from.

\subsection{Partial copying}

The copy gate held, but the policy moved to its edge. The share of outputs
with at least one near-copied source line rose from 8.1\,\% to 44.4\,\%,
although mean copy novelty is still 0.956 and only 3.4\,\% of outputs fall
below 0.8. Part of the content gain comes from this: outputs containing a
copied line have content-word recall 0.382, those without 0.302. The gate
multiplies the content reward by the copied share, so one copied line in
twenty costs only 5\,\% of $R_{\mathrm{content}}$, and can be worth taking
when it raises the line-level F1 by more. Reuse of source phrases below the
gate's threshold is also visible on inspection (``не слетувај'', ``предели
неминати'' carried into an otherwise new poem). This is a modest form of
reward hacking.

\subsection{Training dynamics}

The training reward (at the training weights) rose from 0.248 over the first
50 steps to 0.340 by step 200 and only to 0.361 by step 500. The content term
kept improving slowly after step 200 (0.215 $\to$ 0.244) while copy novelty
and the classifier stayed at their step-200 levels. The step-300 checkpoint
confirms that later steps trade along one frontier: against step 500 it has
lower $R$ (0.381 vs.\ 0.406) and content recall (0.306 vs.\ 0.338), but copies
less (38.7\,\% of outputs with a copied line vs.\ 44.4\,\%; 1.5\,\% below copy
novelty 0.8 vs.\ 3.4\,\%), with the same author identification (top-1 0.088
vs.\ 0.079) and degeneracy (6.6\,\% vs.\ 5.3\,\%). The last 200 steps bought
content recall partly by copying more.

\subsection{The teacher on the same pairs}\label{sec:teacherval}

The teacher column of the v1 table is Gemma~4 31B on the val pairs
themselves (\texttt{teacher\_val.csv}), the upper reference for what the
students imitate. Three observations.

\paragraph{GRPO v1 out-scores its teacher on the reward.} $R=0.406$ against
0.395. The teacher wins the style term by a wide margin (0.686 vs.\ 0.511)
but scores only 0.105 on gated content, below SFT (0.123) and a third of
GRPO's 0.302; its content-word recall (0.159) is below both students'. The
teacher writes a new poem on the source's theme rather than carrying its
lines over, and the line-level content reward scores that as barely above an
unrelated poem. A higher $R$ than the teacher is therefore not a sign that
the student surpassed it; it shows that at $\alpha=\beta=0.5$ the reward
values content preservation the teacher never attempted.

\paragraph{The style gap is the open problem.} On both classifiers the
teacher is identified as the requested author three to eight times as often
as either student (classifier top-1 0.641 vs.\ 0.079--0.158; judge 0.506 vs.\
0.130--0.158). The judge reads the teacher's poems as the target at 76\,\%
of the rate it reads real unseen poems (0.506 vs.\ 0.663). Author identity
is what the teacher has and distillation did not transfer.

\paragraph{The teacher is not degenerate.} 0.2\,\% degenerate outputs, line
uniqueness 0.998: the looping in SFT output is the student's, not inherited
from the data.

\section{GRPO v2: correcting the reward}\label{sec:grpov2}

v1's two failures are both properties of the reward, so v2 changes the
reward and nothing else: same start policy, prompts, hyperparameters, seed
and $\alpha/\beta=0.3/0.7$ (\texttt{slurm/train\_grpo\_v2.slurm},
\texttt{models/qwen3-grpo-v2/}). A v1-vs-v2 difference can therefore be
attributed to the two changes.

\paragraph{Classifier weight 5.6.} The classifier group's within-group
standard deviation is 0.051, against a mean of 0.288 for lexical diversity
and POS bigrams. Weighting it by the ratio, $0.288/0.051\approx5.6$, gives it
the same influence on the group-normalised advantage as either of those
groups, instead of a fifth of it.

\paragraph{Copy gate exponent 3.} The content reward is multiplied by
$\texttt{copy\_novelty}^3$ instead of $\texttt{copy\_novelty}$. One copied
line in twenty then costs $1-0.95^3\approx14\,\%$ of the content reward
instead of 5\,\%, two lines 27\,\% instead of 10\,\%. A fully original poem
is unaffected.

\paragraph{The risk v2 takes.} Raising the classifier's weight makes it the
largest single style term, so v2 is exactly the setting in which the reward
classifier can be gamed; this is why the judge (\S\ref{sec:judge}) exists.
v2 succeeds only if judge top-1 rises with classifier top-1.

\paragraph{The classifier was not gamed.} v2 is the setting in which gaming
was most likely, and the judge rules it out: it moves with the reward
classifier, by less (classifier top-1 $0.079\to0.135$, judge
$0.130\to0.150$), and among the outputs the reward classifier attributes to
the target, the judge agrees on 57\,\% for v2, against 62\,\% for SFT, 54\,\%
for v1 and 64\,\% for the teacher. The character-$n$-gram reward is not being
satisfied by surface tricks a word-level classifier would reject.

\paragraph{The difference from v1 is the majority voices.} Almost all of
v2's gain over v1 is on the two largest authors, Конески ($0.404\to0.638$)
and Пијан Славеј ($0.186\to0.442$), and the share of all outputs the reward
classifier assigns to Конески returns from 21.8\,\% to 35.9\,\%, the SFT
level. The other eight targets stay at 0.000--0.085 on both classifiers, as
they were under SFT and v1. Writing like the corpus's largest author is the
cheapest way to keep the classifier term up, and weighting that term by 5.6
held the policy there. Neither GRPO run beats SFT on author identification by
either measure.

\paragraph{The repetition fix was given back.} Degenerate outputs rise from
5.3\,\% (v1) to 25.4\,\%, close to SFT's 30.1\,\%. v1's repetition fix came
from the lexical-diversity and POS-bigram groups (\S\ref{sec:grporesults});
at classifier weight 5.6 those groups' share of the style reward falls from
2/6 to 2/10.6, and their pull on the policy with it.

\paragraph{The steeper copy gate partly worked.} Outputs with a copied line
fall from 44.4\,\% to 33.8\,\%, and those below copy novelty 0.8 from
3.4\,\% to 2.1\,\%, still well above SFT's 8.1\,\% and 0.4\,\%. Content-word
recall falls with them ($0.338\to0.270$; 0.320 in outputs with a copied line,
0.244 without), confirming that part of v1's content gain was copying.

\paragraph{Why reweighting cannot fix the collapse.} GRPO's update is driven
by differences \emph{within} a group of four samples for the same prompt. For
eight of the ten targets, the student's samples are almost never read as the
target author by either classifier, before or after GRPO, so all four score
near zero on that term and the group-normalised advantage has nothing to
amplify, however large its weight. The only targets on which the classifier
term varies within a group are the ones the student already writes like ---
the two largest authors --- and that is where both GRPO runs moved. The
teacher shows the voices are reachable (judge top-1 0.506 against the 0.663
real-poem ceiling), but that capacity did not survive distillation into a 4B
student from 1{,}146 examples. A student whose SFT distribution already puts
some probability on the minority voices is the precondition for GRPO to help;
\S\ref{sec:gemma} tests whether a different 4B model provides it.

\paragraph{The training log agrees.} Over training
(Figure~\ref{fig:grpocurves}, first versus last 50 steps), v2's classifier
term stays flat, $0.106\to0.103$, where v1's fell, $0.102\to0.078$. The
5.6 weight did not move the policy \emph{towards} the requested authors; it
stopped it moving away from the voices it already had. Lexical diversity
($0.48\to0.56$, against v1's $0.49\to0.71$) and POS bigrams ($0.63\to0.72$,
against $0.64\to0.84$) rose far less than in v1, which is the repetition fix
given back. The ungated content term rose almost identically in both runs
($0.12\to0.24$ and $0.12\to0.25$); the cubed gate kept more of the trained
completions copy-free (copy novelty 0.994 at the end, against 0.990).

\section{A second student: Gemma 4 E4B}\label{sec:gemma}

\subsection{Purpose}

Both of Qwen's open problems --- collapse onto the largest authors and weak
author identification after GRPO --- could be properties of Qwen3-4B rather
than of the data and reward. A second student of matched size from another
family separates the two: if it collapses the same way, the cause is the
method; if it does markedly better, the base model matters, and the size of
the difference says by how much. Only the student changes --- the same
1{,}146 synthetic examples, task format, hyperparameters, GRPO-v2 reward, 468
val pairs and seeds.

\texttt{google/gemma-4-E4B-it} (about 4.5B effective parameters, 8B with
per-layer embeddings, instruction-tuned, Apache 2.0) is the closest match to
Qwen3-4B. It is the teacher's own family, which may make the teacher's
distribution easier to imitate; this is a stated confound, and also the
realistic deployment choice.

\subsection{Implementation}

Everything family-specific lives in one registry,
\texttt{model\_families.py} (hub name, output-directory tag, LoRA targets,
loss masking, loader, decoding overrides). The SFT, merge, generation, GRPO
and reward-inspection scripts take \texttt{--family \{qwen3,gemma4\}}; the
default resolves to exactly the pre-existing Qwen values, which tests pin, so
v1 and v2 stay reproducible. Adapters and merged models record their family,
and loading one into the other family is refused. Facts that forced changes,
verified against the real E4B configuration:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Loader.} \texttt{AutoModelForCausalLM} returns the multimodal
    class with vision and audio towers, so Gemma is loaded text-only
    (\texttt{Gemma4ForCausalLM} with the text config). The hub checkpoint
    stores the language weights under \texttt{model.language\_model.*}, and
    transformers 5.9 has no conversion for it: a plain text-only
    \texttt{from\_pretrained} \emph{silently} leaves every language weight
    at its random initialisation. The loader maps the prefix, disables the
    library's weight-name conversion (which otherwise makes
    \texttt{save\_pretrained} write keys that lose every weight on reload),
    and raises on any missing weight; a test exercises this on a tiny
    checkpoint of the real architecture.
  \item \emph{LoRA targets.} The towers reuse the names
    \texttt{q\_proj}\ldots\texttt{down\_proj} for wrapper modules PEFT cannot
    adapt, so the suffix list Qwen uses is safe only text-only; the
    multimodal fallback uses a regex restricted to the language model (258
    modules over 42 layers --- the last 18 share KV and have no
    \texttt{k\_proj}/\texttt{v\_proj}). The per-layer-embedding projections
    are never targets, for parity with Qwen's attention+MLP set.
  \item \emph{Loss masking.} TRL ships no Gemma~4 training template, so
    \texttt{assistant\_only\_loss} is unavailable. Gemma trains on
    prompt--completion rows with \texttt{completion\_only\_loss}. Gemma's
    \texttt{eos\_token} is \texttt{<eos>} but its turn ends with
    \texttt{<turn|>}, so the end token is set explicitly to
    \texttt{<turn|>}: otherwise SFT would train \texttt{<turn|><eos>}, and
    GRPO, which stops sampling on the tokenizer's end token, would sample
    past the end of the turn. Both formats then put the loss on the poem and
    its end-of-turn token; Qwen's additionally trains its constant empty
    reasoning block, a difference pinned by a test.
  \item \emph{Decoding.} Gemma ships \texttt{top\_k}=64; Gemma runs pin 20 to
    match Qwen.
  \item \emph{Sequence length.} The smallest of 1024, 2048 and 4096 that
    truncates no training example under Gemma's tokenizer. Ten of the 1{,}146
    examples exceed 2048 Gemma tokens, so Gemma trains at 4096 and truncates
    nothing, against Qwen's 6.2\,\% at 1024.
\end{itemize}

A compatibility job (\texttt{gemma\_compat.py}) runs first: load, LoRA target
count, template checks, training-length distribution, one generation, a
3-step SFT with a merge round-trip, and a GRPO smoke run.

The first run (\texttt{data/gemma\_compat\_report.json}) passed five of its
six checks. The text-only loader loaded every language-model weight; the
LoRA targets matched 258 modules (q/o/gate/up/down $\times$ 42, k/v
$\times$ 24); the template's end-of-turn token was detected as
\texttt{<turn|>}, and the untuned model's sample poem ended with it and
contained no reasoning channel; and the length check measured a median of 537
Gemma tokens per training example, 31 over 1024, 10 over 2048 and none over
4096 (maximum 3{,}370), confirming 4096. The SFT--merge round-trip failed:
on one training completion of 323 tokens, the merged model's log-probability
was $-1115.1$ against the adapter model's $-1071.3$ (0.136 per token, against
a tolerance of 0.02), and the GRPO smoke run, which follows it, did not run.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Validation, not test.} Every number here is on the val split,
    which was also used to make decisions (decoding, v2's reward). The test
    split has not been touched; a final run of every system on it is
    the unbiased estimate. \pending{test-split runs, or state explicitly that
    they are out of scope}
  \item \emph{Automatic metrics only.} Author identity is measured by two
    classifiers and fluency by proxies. No human (or native-speaker) rating
    of style, fluency or content has been collected.
  \item \emph{Reward classifier and palettes saw val/test poems.}
    \texttt{classifier.pkl} and the TF-IDF palettes were fit on the whole
    corpus. They are author-level and never see the target of a transfer,
    but they are a leak path; the judge is the clean measurement.
  \item \emph{No held-out SFT loss.} The SFT run monitored only training
    loss (\S\ref{sec:sfttrain}), so the number of epochs was not chosen by
    validation, and the extent of memorisation in epochs 2--3 is unmeasured.
  \item \emph{Qwen SFT truncation.} 6.2\,\% of Qwen's SFT examples were
    truncated at 1024 tokens and not retrained, to keep both GRPO runs' start
    policy fixed.
  \item \emph{Single seed.} Each training run is one seed; differences of a
    few points between runs (e.g.\ classifier top-1 0.079 vs.\ 0.088 for
    v1's two checkpoints) are within run-to-run noise.
  \item \emph{Content measurement is weak.} Unrelated poems already reach
    line-F1 0.553, and the teacher itself preserves little content-word
    recall (0.176), so the content signal is narrow.
  \item \emph{Another Cyrillic language} (Serbian, Russian) would pass the
    validity gate and score well on multilingual content similarity; not
    observed in any output.
  \item \emph{Same-family teacher} for the Gemma student (\S\ref{sec:gemma}).
\end{itemize}
