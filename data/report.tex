\begin{abstract}
This note documents a validation pass performed on the Macedonian-language
stylometric pipeline after its part-of-speech tagger was migrated from spaCy
(\texttt{mk\_core\_news\_lg}) to CLASSLA-Stanza. It covers (i) how much the
downstream Louvain community structure on the semantic knowledge graph (SKG)
changed as a result of the tagger switch, separated from ordinary
seed-to-seed algorithmic noise, including the diagnosis and subsequent fix
of a community-detection artifact (author-similarity edges alone defining a
13-author community with almost no word support) and the seed-stability
tradeoff that fix introduces; (ii) two correctness bugs found and fixed
during the pass, one in rhyme-ending extraction and one in the style-transfer
candidate-selection fallback tier; and (iii) two metric additions — a
length-normalized lexical-diversity measure and stanza-level structural
features — added to the per-author style profiles.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background: the tagger migration}

The pipeline originally tagged the corpus (2{,}711 Macedonian poems and song
lyrics, 24 authors after the $\geq 5$-poem eligibility filter) with spaCy's
\texttt{mk\_core\_news\_lg} model. This was replaced with CLASSLA-Stanza,
which lemmatizes inflection correctly and additionally exposes MULTEXT-East
\texttt{xpos}/\texttt{feats} morphology, enabling the feats-keyed
morphological lookup used by style transfer. The full pipeline — POS
tagging, TF-IDF profiling, SKG construction and enrichment, embeddings, and
graph analytics — was rerun end to end against the new tags. This note asks:
how much did that rebuild actually change the pipeline's outputs, and were
any of those changes symptoms of pre-existing bugs rather than the tagger
switch itself?

\section{Louvain community stability under the tagger switch}
\label{sec:louvain}

\subsection{Setup}

Author-similarity communities are computed by
\texttt{graph\_analytics.py} via \texttt{community.best\_partition(G,\allowbreak
weight='weight',\allowbreak random\_state=42)} on the SKG
(\texttt{skg\_final.gexf}). Both the pre-switch (spaCy) and post-switch
(CLASSLA) versions of \texttt{graph\_analytics.py} call Louvain with these
exact same parameters, so a direct diff of the two partitions is a fair
comparison, not confounded by a methodology change alongside the tagger
change. The pre-switch graph and analytics output were recoverable from git
history (\texttt{git} commit \texttt{5f03983}, carried forward unchanged by
the pure file-reorganization commit \texttt{363009a}); the post-switch
version is the current repository state (commit \texttt{41bb49e} onward).

\subsection{Direct diff against the pre-switch partition}

Both partitions cover the same 24 authors and both settle on 6 communities.
Comparing author community labels directly (after optimal label alignment)
gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Metric & Value \\
\midrule
Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) & 0.221 \\
Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) & 0.483 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An ARI of $1$ would mean the partitions are identical (up to relabeling); an
ARI near $0$ means the agreement is no better than chance. 0.221 indicates a
substantial reshuffling — but Louvain is itself stochastic in the order it
visits nodes, so this number alone cannot distinguish ``the tagger switch
changed the graph structure'' from ``Louvain would have landed somewhere
this different anyway, even on an unchanged graph, under a different run.''

\subsection{Establishing a noise floor}

To separate the two, Louvain was re-run 30 times on the \emph{current}
(post-switch) graph with \texttt{random\_state} $\in \{0, \dots, 29\}$,
holding the graph itself fixed. Each run was compared to the documented
\texttt{random\_state=42} reference partition:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Comparison & Min & Mean & Median \\
\midrule
Each of 30 seeds vs.\ the $\text{seed}=42$ reference (ARI) & 0.515 & 0.877 & 0.906 \\
All pairs among the 30 seeds (ARI, 60 sampled pairs) & 0.469 & 0.819 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The number of communities Louvain settles on also varies by seed alone (4,
5, or 6 communities across the 30 runs).

\subsection{Conclusion: a real change, not just seed noise}

Seed-only variability on a fixed graph floors at ARI $\approx 0.47$--$0.5$ in
the worst observed case. The pre/post-switch ARI (0.221) sits
\emph{below} that floor. The tagger switch therefore produced a partition
shift larger than ordinary Louvain run-to-run noise would account for — a
genuine, if partial, structural change, not an artifact of re-running the
algorithm once.

The change is concentrated among the ``word-driven'' authors (see
\S\ref{sec:community0} for the exception). Three of the six pre-switch
communities (covering 14 of the 24 authors) dissolved and redistributed
across different post-switch communities — e.g.\ the pre-switch cluster
\{Рацин, Поповски, Коцев, Линдро, Лазаров\} split into four different
post-switch communities. This is the expected effect of a tagger change:
different lemmatization directly perturbs the TF-IDF and co-occurrence edges
those communities are built from.

\subsection{Diagnosis: Community 0, a stable but low-information clique}
\label{sec:community0}

The current partition's largest community (``Community 0'') groups 13 of
the 24 authors around only 2 surviving word nodes:

\begin{quote}
\small
Петре М. Андреевски, Влада Урошевиќ, Блаже Конески, Славко Јаневски, Ацо
Шопов, Матеја Матевски, Богомил Ѓузел, Адем Гајтани, Веле Смилевски, Пијан
Славеј, Ефтим Клетников, Ристо Лазаров, Ива Петреска
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Seed stability.} All $\binom{13}{2} = 78$ pairs of these authors
co-clustered together in \emph{every one} of the 30 seed runs described
above — the single most reproducible feature of the entire partition, far
above the general seed-noise floor.

\paragraph{Why it forms.} Breaking down each author's SKG weighted degree by
edge type shows why: every Community-0 author draws 40--70\% of its
weighted degree from direct \texttt{author\_similarity} edges (author
$\leftrightarrow$ author Jaccard-vocabulary-overlap edges), versus
86--100\% from word-mediated (\texttt{distinctive}/\texttt{cooccurrence})
edges for every author outside the community. Community 0 is not a set of
authors Louvain found to write alike through shared vocabulary; it is the
set of authors whose word nodes did not survive strongly into the graph
(consistent with the SKG's frequency floors and node cap), leaving the
direct author-similarity edges as the dominant signal available to place
them by.

\paragraph{Not new to the tagger switch.} The same phenomenon already
existed pre-switch, with a 9-author core — Андреевски, Урошевиќ, Конески,
Јаневски, Ѓузел, Гајтани, Славеј, Клетников, Петреска — all a subset of
today's 13, each already showing 13--47\% author-similarity weight share
before the tagger changed. The switch grew this core from 9 to 13 authors
(adding Шопов, Матевски, Смилевски, Лазаров), it did not create it.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Community 0 should be read as ``authors the
graph has too little word-level signal to place elsewhere,'' not as
evidence of shared literary style — and, as described next, was subsequently
removed by changing what Louvain is allowed to see.

\subsection{Fix: excluding \texttt{author\_similarity} edges from the
partitioning graph}
\label{sec:community0fix}

\texttt{graph\_analytics.py} now runs Louvain on a copy of the SKG with
every \texttt{author\_similarity} edge removed, keeping
\texttt{cooccurrence}, \texttt{semantic\_similarity}, and
\texttt{distinctive} edges — i.e.\ only edges mediated by an actual word —
as the partitioning signal. \texttt{author\_similarity} edges are still
computed and still present in \texttt{skg\_final.gexf} for every other
purpose (PageRank, weighted degree, visualization); they are excluded only
from the graph Louvain sees, since \S\ref{sec:community0} showed they were
sufficient on their own to define a community with no word-level support
behind it. The direct author-similarity signal remains available where it
was always meant to be used as an auxiliary/fallback source — in
\texttt{candidate\_selection.author\_similarity()}, which already computed
it independently of the graph, for \texttt{tier\_cross\_author\_similar}.

\paragraph{Result.} Community 0 no longer exists. Louvain on the restricted
graph settles on 5 communities instead of 6, and every one now has
substantial word support (49--102 word nodes each, versus the old Community
0's 2). The 13 authors previously grouped by author-similarity edges alone
are now distributed across all 5 communities on genuine vocabulary evidence:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Former Community-0 author & New community (by word evidence) \\
\midrule
Блаже Конески, Славко Јаневски, Ацо Шопов & Community 2 (with Кочо Рацин) \\
Влада Урошевиќ, Пијан Славеј, Ефтим Клетников & Community 1 \\
Петре М. Андреевски, Богомил Ѓузел, Ристо Лазаров, Ива Петреска & Community 3 \\
Адем Гајтани, Веле Смилевски & Community 4 \\
Матеја Матевски & Community 0 (new, unrelated to the old one) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Cost: the new partition is less seed-stable.} Repeating the
30-seed sweep from \S\ref{sec:louvain} on the restricted graph gives a
markedly lower stability floor than the full graph had:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Graph & Min ARI & Mean ARI & Median ARI \\
\midrule
Full graph (incl.\ \texttt{author\_similarity}), \S\ref{sec:louvain} & 0.515 & 0.877 & 0.906 \\
Restricted graph (word-mediated only) & 0.300 & 0.581 & 0.541 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This is the expected price of the fix, not a sign it is wrong: the
author-similarity edges were direct, high-weight author$\leftrightarrow$author
connections that gave Louvain unambiguous structure to lock onto: precisely
the ``glue'' that made Community 0 so artificially stable
(\S\ref{sec:community0}). Without it, the remaining word-mediated graph is
sparser, and Louvain's well-known sensitivity to node-visit order shows up
more. Per-community confidence is uneven: averaging pairwise co-clustering
across the 30 seeds by the $\text{seed}=42$ community gives 0.88 for
Community 4 and 0.74 for Community 3 (high confidence) down to 0.49 for
Community 2 and 0.27 for the 3-author Community 0 (Матевски, Линдро,
Цветкоски — low confidence, on par with a coin flip for some pairs).

\paragraph{Recommendation.} Treat the 5-community, word-mediated partition
as the current best estimate of stylistic community structure, but do not
read the exact $\text{random\_state}=42$ assignment as ground truth for the
weaker communities (2 and the new 0) the way one might have been tempted to
for the old, artifactually-stable Community 0. A seed-ensemble/consensus
partition would give a more defensible per-pair confidence than any single
seed and is a natural next step if community membership starts being used
for a downstream decision (e.g.\ cluster-based candidate pooling) rather
than reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Addendum (poem-id migration).} The table above and its seed=42
assignments were computed before a later fix gave every poem a stable
\texttt{poem\_id}, correcting $\sim$15 \texttt{(author, song\_title)} pairs
in the corpus that had been sharing a title across distinct physical poems
(see \texttt{CLAUDE.md}'s Data quality context). That fix nudges
\texttt{pos\_transitions.json} and the SKG's edge weights by a small,
legitimate amount for the affected authors — among them Блаже Конески,
Ефтим Клетников, Влада Урошевиќ, and Пијан Славеј. Re-running
\texttt{graph\_analytics.py} after the fix shows this small a perturbation
is enough to move Конески out of his former group with Јаневски/Шопов/Рацин
and into the group with Гајтани/Смилевски instead, while Урошевиќ/Славеј/
Клетников and Андреевски/Ѓузел/Лазаров/Петреска remain grouped together
(under new community numbers — Louvain's own labels are not stable across
runs and carry no meaning by themselves). This is not a contradiction of
this section's finding; it is a further, unplanned demonstration of it:
this restricted graph's community structure is sensitive enough that even a
small, targeted correctness fix elsewhere in the pipeline reshuffles the
weaker communities. Properly updating the table above would mean repeating
the full 30-seed sweep on the corrected graph, not just swapping in one new
run's numbers — that re-verification is out of scope for the poem-id
migration and is a natural next step before this section's exact figures
are relied on again.

\section{Correctness fixes found during validation}

Two bugs, unrelated to the tagger switch, were found and fixed while
validating pipeline outputs.

\subsection{Rhyme-ending extraction}

\texttt{style\_profiler.py}'s \texttt{top\_rhyme\_endings} field took the
literal last three characters of each line's last whitespace-split token,
with no punctuation stripping. Macedonian poetry in this corpus frequently
ends lines on a standalone em/en dash (a caesura marker) or on punctuation
glued to the last word, so every one of the 24 authors' top-5 rhyme endings
were contaminated with punctuation artifacts — e.g.\ Блаже Конески's were
\texttt{--|на,|на.|ам.|ат.} and Кочо Рацин's were
\texttt{--|---|ата|ите|ни,}. This was corpus-wide, not an isolated case.

The fix extracts a genuine line-final syllable: it walks backward past
trailing pure-punctuation tokens, strips edge punctuation from the real
last word, and returns the substring from the onset of the final vowel's
syllable (the character after the second-to-last vowel) through the end of
the word. After the fix, no author's top rhyme endings contain punctuation,
and the endings reflect real Macedonian grammatical suffixes (\texttt{-та},
\texttt{-те}, \texttt{-на}, \texttt{-ва}, and similar).

\subsection{Style-transfer legacy-fallback tier}

\texttt{candidate\_selection.py}'s last-resort candidate tier
(\texttt{tier\_legacy\_fallback}) imported a hardcoded
\texttt{TARGET\_AUTHOR} constant from \texttt{llm\_probe.py} instead of
using the \texttt{target\_author} parameter it was already given, so
whenever this tier fired for any author other than that hardcoded default,
it silently supplied \emph{that other author's} TF-IDF vocabulary as
replacement candidates. The fix threads \texttt{target\_author} through to
\texttt{load\_target\_words()}, which now accepts an explicit author rather
than reading the module constant.

To measure real-world impact, \texttt{style\_transfer.transfer\_style()}
was re-run over 960 sampled (source poem, target author) pairs spanning all
24 authors, instrumented to record which tier produced each of the 20{,}940
replacement slots evaluated:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Tier of the candidate chosen in the output & Share of slots \\
\midrule
\texttt{primary} & 94.20\% \\
\texttt{relaxed\_semantic} & 5.73\% \\
\texttt{legacy\_fallback} & 0.062\% (13 slots) \\
\texttt{cross\_author\_similar} & 0.014\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The bug was real wherever it fired, but it fired rarely — 13 of 20{,}940
slots, concentrated in a handful of sparse-vocabulary authors (8 of the 13
for a single author, Јосип Коцев). It was corrupting a rare edge case, not a
meaningful share of style-transfer output.

\section{Metric additions to the author style profiles}

\subsection{Length-normalized lexical diversity}

Plain type-token ratio (TTR) in \texttt{author\_style\_profiles.csv} is not
length-normalized, and per-author corpus size varies 37-fold across the 24
authors (232 to 8{,}538 content tokens). On this corpus, plain TTR
correlates with token count at Pearson $r = -0.76$ ($p = 1.7\times10^{-5}$)
— the expected artifact of TTR mechanically falling as a corpus grows,
rather than a signal of authorial lexical richness. A Moving-Average
Type-Token Ratio (MATTR; Covington and McFall, 2010) column was added,
averaging the TTR of every 200-token sliding window over each author's
lemma sequence (window size chosen to fit inside the smallest eligible
author's 232-token corpus). MATTR against token count: $r = -0.037$
($p = 0.86$) — no residual length dependence. The original \texttt{type\_
token\_ratio} column is retained for reference but is now documented as
diagnostic-only; \texttt{mattr} is the column to use for cross-author
comparison.

\subsection{Stanza-level structure}

\texttt{author\_style\_profiles.csv} previously had no stanza-level
features. Poem text in \texttt{stripped\_songs.csv} uses blank-line-delimited
stanzas (the same convention already relied on elsewhere in the pipeline for
stanza extraction). Two fields were added, computed per author from that
delimiting: \texttt{avg\_stanzas\_per\_poem} and
\texttt{avg\_lines\_per\_stanza}. Values are plausible and vary
meaningfully across authors — e.g.\ Ристо Лазаров averages 7.23
stanzas/poem at 2.95 lines/stanza (short, couplet-like stanzas), while
Лилјана Дирјан averages 1.14 stanzas/poem at 15.62 lines/stanza (long,
largely unbroken poems).

\section{Worked example: style transfer after the fixes}
\label{sec:example}

As a concrete illustration of where the pipeline stands after the fixes in
this note, this section walks through one full style-transfer run on the
current (post-fix) pipeline state: Кочо Рацин's ``Балада за непознатиот''
(``Ballad of the Unknown [Soldier]''), rewritten in Блаже Конески's style
via \texttt{style\_transfer.transfer\_style(text, `Блаже Конески')}. This is
a real, reproducible pipeline run, not a hand-picked or edited example — it
was independently regenerated against the current corpus and model files
and diffs byte-identical (modulo typographic quote-mark style) to the run
quoted below.

\begin{quote}
\small
\ttfamily
Натаму – в поле битолско\\
чемрее врба проклета –\\
под врбата незнаен гроб,\\
в гроб лежи војник непознат.\\[0.3em]
Лежи од војна света,\\
лежи — и веќе земјосал —\\
силно го тага гласи\\
мрзне тука загинал.\\[0.3em]
Никој крај него немаше –\\
вишното небе врз него,\\
земјата скришна под него,\\
над гробот врба спремам.\\[0.3em]
{\normalfont(\dots)}\\[0.3em]
И од ним – самовилите\\
една по една идеа –\\
од гроб до гроб го мрзне\\
јунак до јунак – на оро.\\[0.3em]
{\normalfont(\dots)}
\end{quote}

(Full output omitted here for space; see the corpus entry for the source
text and re-run \texttt{style\_transfer.transfer\_style} for the complete
transferred poem.)

\paragraph{What this demonstrates, and what it doesn't.} This call did not
pass \texttt{source\_author}, so only replacement criterion (b) — a lemma
absent from the target's vocabulary for a POS in
\texttt{B\_ELIGIBLE\_POS} $=\{$ADJ, ADV, VERB$\}$ — is active; criterion (a)
(source-distinctive/target-absent, any content POS) needs a source author
and did not fire here. This is visible directly in the output: NOUN tokens
like \texttt{гроб} (`grave') and \texttt{гробишта} (`graveyard') are
preserved verbatim throughout, exactly as \texttt{style\_transfer.py}'s own
design comment (labelled ``Criterion (b)'') intends — nouns carry subject
matter, not style, and are deliberately left alone under this criterion. Adjectives and verbs are substituted where
Конески's own vocabulary lacks the Рацин original: \texttt{војна
световна} (`worldly/mundane war') becomes \texttt{војна света} (`holy
war'), and \texttt{тага изела} (`grief consumed [him]') becomes
\texttt{тага гласи} (`grief speaks/sounds'), each drawing a real
Конески-attested word of the same POS into the same grammatical slot.

\paragraph{Two honest limitations visible in this same output.} First,
\texttt{задека} (`because') — a conjunction — is itself replaced (with
\texttt{мрзне}, `freezes'), which looks wrong until you check the tagging:
CLASSLA tags this token \texttt{VERB} in this context
(\texttt{Aspect=Perf|Tense=Past}), not \texttt{CONJ}/\texttt{SCONJ}, so it
passes through \texttt{CONTENT\_POS} and becomes a legitimate replacement
target under the (mistaken) tag. This is the same category of tagging
error \texttt{pos\_flags.csv}'s \texttt{upos\_xpos\_clash}/\texttt{unstable\_
tag} reasons are built to catch for common cases — \texttt{задека} was not
one already in \texttt{data/pos\_corrections.csv} at the time of this run,
so it slipped through uncorrected. Second, \texttt{дигаа} (`they raised',
\texttt{Number=Plur}, 3rd-person imperfect) is replaced by \texttt{мрзне},
which surfaces in an unmarked/singular-present-looking form — the
morphological lookup evidently had no attested Конески plural-imperfect
form of that lemma to realise into, and fell back to a lower morph tier
(\texttt{nearest\_attested} or \texttt{unresolved}) rather than the
requested agreement. Neither of these breaks the pipeline, and neither is
new to this session's fixes — they are exactly the kind of residual,
low-frequency noise the tier/morph-tier logging in
\texttt{candidate\_selection.py} exists to make visible and auditable,
rather than silent.

\section{Prompt-construction fixes in the LLM style-transfer
  pipeline}\label{sec:llmprompt}

A second, independent style-transfer approach (\texttt{llm\_style\_transfer.py})
sends a single LLM prompt — an LLM-written summary of the source poem, its
content keywords, the target author's style profile in prose, a
relevance-filtered vocabulary palette, tone exemplar lines, and structural
targets — and asks the model to write a new poem, rather than substituting
tokens into masked slots. Auditing the per-run records it writes to
\texttt{data/llm\_transfer\_logs/} surfaced three defects in how that prompt
was assembled. None of them raised an error; all of them degraded the prompt
silently.

\subsection{Duplicate exemplar lines}

The tone-exemplar block repeated lines verbatim. In a Ацо Шопов run, four of
nine exemplars were two duplicated pairs (\texttt{Треба да бидеме подобри.}
and \texttt{Вечер е тиха, дремлива,}), and a further two came from a single
poem. The block is introduced to the model as a rhythm reference, so a
verbatim repeat inside it is an instruction to repeat; the generated poem
duplicated whole stanzas and repeated single lines three times.

Duplicates entered by two routes. First, \texttt{exemplar\_selection.py}'s
\texttt{qualifying\_lines()} capped candidates at two lines per poem but did
not check whether they were the same text, so a refrain spent the entire
quota on one line — \texttt{Треба да бидеме подобри} opens with that line
twice (\texttt{poem\_id} 88), and \texttt{Партизанска колона} repeats
\texttt{Вечер е тиха, дремлива,} at lines 2 and 21 (\texttt{poem\_id} 50).
Because identical text yields an identical line embedding, both copies always
landed in the same k-means cluster and the two-per-cluster rule selected both.
Second, the corpus's near-duplicate poems each contributed the same line
independently: Блаже Конески's \texttt{Бура} (\texttt{poem\_id} 13) and
\texttt{БУРА} (\texttt{poem\_id} 566) are one poem under two title casings,
and 24 such author/lowercased-title groups exist.

The fix de-duplicates normalized line text (case-folded, whitespace-collapsed)
author-wide rather than per-poem, which closes both routes; a repeat is
skipped without consuming a per-poem slot, so a refrain poem still contributes
two distinct lines. Separately, \texttt{cluster\_and\_select()} now enforces
one line per source poem globally across clusters rather than within them,
and processes clusters smallest-first so that a constrained cluster claims its
poem before a roomier one takes it. Across the corpus's 38 authors this costs
7 exemplar lines in total (218 to 211) and no author falls below 5. Exemplar
records now carry \texttt{poem\_id} alongside \texttt{song\_title}, since the
title alone cannot distinguish \texttt{Бура} from \texttt{БУРА} in a run log.

\subsection{Summary language}

Summaries were requested in Macedonian and returned ungrammatical: the logged
summary for Кочо Рацин's \texttt{Балада за непознатиот} opened
\texttt{Песната приказува} (for \texttt{прикажува}), and continued
\texttt{го тага неговото загињување} and \texttt{го поседуваат несигурниот
смисол на нивната смрт} — text that conveys the gist but is not well-formed
Macedonian. The local model in use (\texttt{qwen3:14b}) reads Macedonian
considerably better than it writes it, and the summary is scaffolding
consumed by the generation step, not output shown to a reader.

Summaries are therefore now taken in English. The prompt's lexical material —
content keywords, vocabulary palette, exemplar lines — and the generated poem
itself remain Macedonian; only the description of the source poem's content
changes language. This matches the existing choice to phrase instructions in
English (\texttt{DEFAULT\_INSTR\_LANG}). \texttt{SUMMARY\_PROMPT\_VERSION} was
bumped to 2, which invalidates cached v1 summaries via the cache key.

\subsection{Unstated output language}

The prompt never instructed the model which language to write in. Under v1
this went unnoticed because the Macedonian summary was the largest
Cyrillic block in the prompt and the model followed it by example. Moving the
summary to English removed that implicit cue, so the instruction is now
explicit (\emph{``Write the poem in Macedonian, in Cyrillic script''}). The
same audit found that \texttt{--lang mk} was an advertised CLI choice with no
corresponding entry in \texttt{PROMPT\_TEMPLATE}, so it raised
\texttt{KeyError} during prompt assembly; the choices are now derived from the
templates that actually exist.

\subsection{A lemmatization error surfaced by the prompt}

Reading the assembled prompts also caught a tagging error that no flag rule
raises. The content keywords for Кочо Рацин's \texttt{Балада за непознатиот}
included \texttt{петлин}, which is not a Macedonian word: classla lemmatizes
the definite plural \texttt{петлите} to \texttt{петлин} rather than
\texttt{петел} (`rooster'). It gets every other form of the word right —
\texttt{петел}, \texttt{петелот} and \texttt{петли} all lemmatize to
\texttt{петел} — so the corpus carried one lemma split across two spellings,
6 tokens under the wrong one, across three authors (Кочо Рацин, Милош Линдро,
Ефтим Клетников).

Nothing in \texttt{pos\_flags.csv} catches this: the rules look at tags, not at
lemma consistency, and the POS and \texttt{xpos} (\texttt{Ncmpny}) were both
correct here. The fix is a lemma-only entry in
\texttt{data/pos\_corrections.csv} — blank \texttt{correction}, \texttt{lemma\_
fix} of \texttt{петел}, corpus-wide scope — which \texttt{corrections.apply\_to}
applies without clearing \texttt{xpos}/\texttt{feats}, since no POS judgement
was overruled.

The effect on the prompt is not simply a substituted keyword. Merging the 6
tokens into \texttt{петел}'s existing count raises that lemma's corpus
frequency, lowering its IDF, and it consequently drops out of the poem's top-10
keywords altogether — the previous ranking was inflated by a spurious singleton
lemma. Lemma-keyed artifacts further downstream (\texttt{word\_embeddings.json},
the SKG, \texttt{morph\_lookup.json}) still carry a stale \texttt{петлин} entry;
at 6 tokens in 70{,}531 it was not judged worth a full rebuild, and
\texttt{петел} already has an embedding, so the keyword remains embeddable.

\subsection{Switching the generation model}\label{sec:gemmaswitch}

All of the prompt work above was developed and logged against
\texttt{qwen3:14b} running locally under Ollama. With the prompt defects fixed,
that model was the remaining constraint on output quality. It writes
Macedonian poorly: generated poems repeated whole stanzas, ran single lines
three times (\texttt{и се вика: ``Иде, иде, иде, иде, иде.''}), and produced
non-words and broken agreement (\texttt{кое се вик}, \texttt{каде се вод}).

The pipeline was therefore switched to \texttt{gemma-4-31b-it}, a
substantially larger model with much stronger Macedonian. It is now the
default (\texttt{DEFAULT\_MODEL}/\texttt{DEFAULT\_BACKEND} in
\texttt{llm\_style\_transfer.py}); Ollama and \texttt{qwen3:14b} remain
reachable through \texttt{--backend ollama --model qwen3:14b}, so the earlier
logs stay reproducible.

Two practical obstacles shaped how it is reached. First, every OpenRouter
\texttt{:free} Gemma route answers HTTP 429 from a shared upstream pool at
Google AI Studio, so the model is called through Google's Generative Language
API directly, with its own key — a third \texttt{google} backend in
\texttt{llm\_client.py} alongside \texttt{ollama} and \texttt{openrouter}.
Second, Gemma~4 always reasons before answering, and cannot be stopped from
doing so: \texttt{generationConfig.thinkingConfig.thinkingBudget} is rejected
with \texttt{400 Thinking budget is not supported for this model}. The
scratchpad returns as a response part flagged \texttt{thought=true} beside the
answer part, so it has to be filtered out — concatenating every part yields the
model arguing with itself rather than the poem.

Thinking tokens are charged against \texttt{maxOutputTokens}, and their number
varies enough between runs of the \emph{same} prompt that no fixed budget is
safe: observed figures for one style-transfer prompt were 3231 thought tokens
(305 for the poem), then 15{,}997 with no answer produced at all, then 2075 on
a re-send. The backend therefore requests the model's full 32{,}768-token
output ceiling and retries once when a response comes back with thinking but no
answer part, rather than attempting to budget around the variance.

\subsection{Effect}

Re-running both logged transfers from the same source poem confirmed the
prompt-level fixes: exemplar blocks now contain no repeated line and no
repeated source poem (8 lines/8 poems for Ацо Шопов, 10/10 for Блаже Конески),
and the summary is fluent, accurate English.

The model switch is visible in the structural fit, which was measured but never
targeted by either change:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Structural target (same prompt, same source poem) & \texttt{qwen3:14b} &
\texttt{gemma-4-31b-it} \\
\midrule
Ацо Шопов, lines (target 20.7) & 16 & 22 \\
Ацо Шопов, $\Delta$ tokens per line & $+0.99$ & $+0.10$ \\
Блаже Конески, lines (target 16.4) & 14 & 16 \\
Блаже Конески, stanzas (target 2.3) & 2 & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The qualitative gap is larger than the table suggests. Gemma places the target
author's palette words in grammatical context rather than stacking them —
\texttt{тишината личи на тешка небиднина} uses Шопов's own coinage
\texttt{небиднина} in a well-formed clause — and for Конески it produced a
strict $8+8$ poem built on a consistent adjective--adverb--verb line template
(\texttt{студена коска тесно го стега}, \texttt{чемреена мака бавно го јаде}),
which is the adjective-before-noun pattern that author's style profile
describes.

Content preservation improved as well, and traceably so: the English summary
identifies that the dead soldier \emph{refuses} to join the heroes' oro because
he lacks their honour. That refusal is the source poem's central turn, it is
absent from the Macedonian v1 summary, and it appears in both regenerated
poems.

as of commit \texttt{41bb49e} (or later, up to the fix below, on branch

\S\ref{sec:community0fix} (the fix) were produced against

branch, which restricts Louvain's input graph to word-mediated edges; commit

produced by one-off scripts run against the live pipeline rather than
checked-in tests, since they characterize algorithmic stability and
empirical tier frequency rather than fixed expected behavior.
